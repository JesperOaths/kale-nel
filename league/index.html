Report coverage & sources

Trust & coverage

How much evidence supports this report?

Understand the numbersPercentage points, uncertainty, standardized gaps and the game sample
Percentage points · pp

Subtract two percentages directly. If kill participation rises from 50% to 60%, the change is +10 percentage points. That is a 20% relative increase, a different calculation. This report spells the difference out as “points”.

Standardized gap · Hedges’ g

An average win/loss difference divided by the metric’s typical within-group spread, with a small-sample correction. It lets CS, damage and deaths share a scale. Zero means no average gap; a size near 1 is roughly one typical spread. In the chart, right means more favorable in wins; lower-is-better metrics are flipped. It is neither a percentage nor a predicted chance of winning.

Method: NIST standardized mean differences.

Uncertainty range · Wilson interval

The dot is the observed rate. The line shows a 95% confidence interval based on the success and opportunity counts. For 10 wins in 20 games, the observed rate is 50% and the range is about 30%–70%. A wide line means less precision. Ranking uses the lower end to avoid putting tiny lucky samples first. It does not promise a future win rate. Encounters in the same game can be linked, so event-based ranges are approximate.

Method: NIST proportion confidence intervals.

Game sample · cohort

The games actually used for a calculation. Deep coaching uses the latest eligible 20 games in the selected role and queue, with the report’s mechanics rules applied. History can use more selected-role games from the 100-match account scan. An ADC review does not borrow your Top games. “n” is the valid game count for that particular metric; opportunity counts are separate.

Typical game · median, quartiles & IQR

The median is the middle value after sorting the games. Q25 and Q75 mark the edges of the middle 50%; the IQR is the distance between them. A narrower middle range means less variation. For damage, a lower Q25 describes weaker games; for deaths, a higher Q75 describes the worse end. These values are less affected by one extreme game than an average.

Meaningful-change scale · 1×

Each metric has a practical threshold chosen by the analyzer, such as 150 gold or 50 damage per minute. A +2× bar means a favorable change twice that threshold, not twice the performance. These thresholds are coaching rules, not tests of statistical significance. The exact averages remain available below the chart.

Comparable results · clean outcomes

Win-rate comparisons exclude the outcomes tagged as AFK or early surrender, and unverified results where applicable. “Clean” describes the result sample; it does not mean the player made no mistakes. The result strip still shows excluded games with a separate marker.

Your role opponent · direct peer

The enemy player assigned to the same role in that match. Direct comparisons require a trusted role match. Population rank references use an external collection of ranked games instead; those references are approximate and are labeled separately.

Deaths per 10 minutes · exposure

Divide the deaths in a phase by the total minutes actually played in that phase, then multiply by 10. One death in 20 minutes gives 0.5 per 10 minutes. This keeps a long late game from looking worse just because it lasted longer. Risky, costly and severe deaths can overlap, so do not add those bars.

CS, DPM, KP, KDA & vision

CS/min = minions and monsters killed per minute. DPM = champion damage per minute. KP = the share of team kills you helped with. KDA = (kills + assists) divided by deaths, using the report’s zero-death handling. Vision/min measures vision-score volume; it does not by itself show whether a ward was useful.

A pattern is a review clue

A statistic can differ between wins and losses because of opponents, game length, champion picks or changing game states. It does not show that raising that number will cause a win. Review the linked games and test one practical change. Missing measurements stay unknown rather than being counted as zero.
